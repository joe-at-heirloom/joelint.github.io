Privacy

Last updated November 22, 2026

Inkblotter collects nothing you draw or type, and has no account and no sign-in. Since version 1.1 it is free, and now and then, between one blot and the next, it shows an advertisement from Google AdMob. Google’s software collects some information from your device to do that, as described under Advertisements. Remove Ads, or having bought the app before it went free, turns the ads off, and that collection with them.

Inkblotter is published by The Peninsular Company, LLC, a Michigan limited liability company based in Detroit.

What stays on your device

  • The ink you drop, the blot it makes and what you type about it stay on your iPhone or iPad. Inkblotter doesn’t save them: starting a new blot clears the last one.
  • What you type about the ten plates in The Ten Plates stays on your device too, and is gone when you close the book.
  • Readings are written on your device. Where Apple Intelligence is available, Inkblotter uses Apple’s on-device model; elsewhere it uses text built into the app. Nothing you type is sent to us or to anyone else.

Sharing

If you tap Share on a reading, you choose where the image goes using the iOS share sheet. We never see what you share or where it goes.

Advertisements

The advertisements come from Google AdMob. To show one, count that it was seen and guard against fraud, Google’s software in the app collects:

  • an identifier for your device: Apple’s identifier for vendors, and Apple’s advertising identifier only if you have allowed apps to track you. Inkblotter never asks for that;
  • your IP address, from which a rough location is worked out;
  • which advertisements were shown and how you interacted with them, and diagnostic information such as crashes and loading times.

The advertisements are not personalized: Inkblotter asks Google not to choose them from what it knows about you, and the app does not track you across other companies’ apps and websites. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google asks for your consent first, and you can change your answer at any time under Privacy choices on the app’s About page. Google handles this information under its own privacy policy; How Google uses information from apps that use its services explains more.

Buying the app, and Remove Ads

Inkblotter cost $2.99 until November 2026; it is now free, and Remove Ads is a one-time purchase inside it. Apple handles both: it takes the payment and does not give us your name, your Apple Account or your payment details. To know whether to show advertisements, the app asks Apple, on your device, whether this Apple Account bought the app or Remove Ads; the answer stays on your device. Apple tells us how many copies and purchases were sold and in which countries. If you have chosen in your device’s settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple also sends us crash reports and usage figures that do not identify you.

If you write to us

When you email inkblotter@thepeninsularcompany.com, we receive your email address, your name if your email includes it, your message and any attachments. We use them to answer you and to keep a record of our conversation. Our email hosting provider stores these messages for us. We do not sell them.

We keep email for three years after our last contact. You can ask us at any time what we hold about you, to correct it or to delete it: write to the same address.

This website

These pages live on joelint.com. They use Google Analytics to count visits and see which pages are read: Google’s script sets cookies in your browser and collects your IP address, your browser and device, and the pages you view, and handles them under its privacy policy. The pages carry no advertising and no forms. They are hosted by GitHub Pages, which may log your IP address and the details of your request for security and operations.

Children

Inkblotter is not directed at children. It collects nothing anyone draws or types; what the advertisements collect is described above.

Changes
