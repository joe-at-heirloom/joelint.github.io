How to convert M4A to MP3 on a Mac

Voice Memos, GarageBand exports and a lot of downloaded audio arrive as M4A. Some car stereos, older players and upload forms only take MP3. Your Mac can convert them without uploading anything.

With the Music app

  1. In Music, choose Music › Settings › Files › Import Settings (Preferences on older macOS).
  2. Set Import Using to MP3 Encoder, pick a quality, and click OK.
  3. Add the M4A files to your library if they aren't there already, and select them.
  4. Choose File › Convert › Create MP3 Version.

The MP3s go into your Music library, alongside the originals. If you just want the files and not a library full of voice memos, that's the catch.

With The Converter

The Converter is a free, open-source Mac app that converts images, video, audio and documents entirely on your Mac.

  1. Drop the M4A files, or a folder of them, onto The Converter's window.
  2. Pick MP3.
  3. Click Convert. Each MP3 is saved next to its original; nothing is added to your Music library.

Or skip the window: select the files in Finder, Control-click, and choose Services › Convert to MP3. The new file lands next to the original.

What you get

  • Variable-bitrate MP3 at LAME's V0 on the default High quality setting, around 245 kbps on average. Balanced uses V2 (about 190 kbps) and Smaller files V5 (about 130 kbps).
  • No file-size or length limit, so a two-hour recording is fine.

The Converter is free and open source. It needs macOS 14 or later on an Apple Silicon Mac (M1 or newer).

Questions

Does converting M4A to MP3 lose quality?

Both are lossy formats, so converting one to the other always costs a little. At V0 it's very hard to hear. If you want no loss at all, convert to FLAC or WAV instead.

Why won't some songs convert?

Songs downloaded from Apple Music, and old iTunes purchases saved as .m4p, are copy-protected. No converter can open them.

Can it convert Voice Memos?

Yes. Drag a memo out of the Voice Memos app into a Finder folder, then convert that file.

Are my files uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything happens on your Mac.
