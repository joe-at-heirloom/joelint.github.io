How to convert HEIC to JPG on a Mac

iPhones save photos as HEIC, and plenty of websites, forms and Windows PCs still want JPG. You don't need to upload anything to convert them. Finder and Preview can do a few photos; The Converter does whole folders and keeps each photo's color, date and location.

With what's already on your Mac

Finder, for a handful of photos

  1. Select the HEIC files in Finder.
  2. Control-click them and choose Quick Actions › Convert Image.
  3. Set the format to JPEG, leave the size at Actual Size to keep every pixel, and click Convert to JPEG.

Convert Image is in macOS Monterey and later. The JPGs appear next to the originals.

Preview, if you want to set the quality

  1. Open the photo in Preview.
  2. Choose File › Export.
  3. Set Format to JPEG, move the Quality slider, and click Save.

For several photos, open them together, select them all in Preview's sidebar and use File › Export Selected Images. Click Options to pick JPEG.

With The Converter

The Converter is a free, open-source Mac app that converts images, video, audio and documents entirely on your Mac.

  1. Drop the photos, or a whole folder of them, onto The Converter's window.
  2. Pick JPEG.
  3. Click Convert. Each JPG is saved next to its HEIC, unless you've chosen another folder in Settings.

Or skip the window: select the files in Finder, Control-click, and choose Services › Convert to JPEG. The new file lands next to the original.

What you get

  • Quality 90 on the default High quality setting (82 on Balanced, 70 on Smaller files).
  • The color profile, resolution and EXIF data come along, including the date taken and the location.
  • Rotation is baked into the pixels, so a photo taken sideways shows the right way up everywhere.
  • To leave the location and camera details out, turn on Remove location and camera info from converted images in Settings.

The Converter is free and open source. It needs macOS 14 or later on an Apple Silicon Mac (M1 or newer).

Questions

Why is the JPG bigger than the HEIC?

HEIC packs the same picture into roughly half the space of a JPG. That's why iPhones use it. Converting doesn't add detail; the JPG just needs more bytes to hold it.

Do I lose quality converting HEIC to JPG?

A little, as with any JPG. At quality 90 it's very hard to see. If you need every pixel exactly as it was, convert to PNG or TIFF instead; they're lossless and much larger.

How do I stop my iPhone saving HEIC?

On the iPhone, go to Settings › Camera › Formats and choose Most Compatible. New photos are then saved as JPG. Photos you've already taken stay HEIC.

Are my photos uploaded anywhere?

No. The Converter runs entirely on your Mac and works with the Wi-Fi off. Its only network call is a weekly check for a new version.
