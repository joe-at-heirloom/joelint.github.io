How to convert a PDF to Word on a Mac

macOS has no built-in way to turn a PDF into an editable Word document. Most of the tools that do it want you to upload the PDF to their servers first. The Converter does it on your Mac.

With what's already on your Mac

The closest thing is copying and pasting: open the PDF in Preview, select the text, copy it, and paste it into Word or Pages. That's fine for a paragraph or two. For a long document you lose the formatting and spend a while putting it back.

With The Converter

The Converter is a free, open-source Mac app that converts images, video, audio and documents entirely on your Mac.

  1. Drop the PDF onto The Converter's window.
  2. Pick Word (.docx). RTF is there too.
  3. Click Convert. The .docx is saved next to the PDF.

Or skip the window: select the files in Finder, Control-click, and choose Services › Convert to Word. The new file lands next to the original.

What you get

  • The text of every page, with its fonts, sizes, bold and italics, in one editable .docx.
  • The layout isn't kept: columns, text boxes and exact positions come out as flowing text. It's built for getting the words out so you can edit them.
  • It doesn't need Word installed. The .docx opens in Word, Pages or Google Docs.

The Converter is free and open source. It needs macOS 14 or later on an Apple Silicon Mac (M1 or newer).

Questions

Why did my PDF come out empty?

It's probably a scan. A scanned PDF is a picture of each page with no text in it, so there's nothing to pull out. The Converter doesn't do OCR (reading text out of pictures).

Is it safe for confidential documents?

Yes. The PDF never leaves your Mac, which is the point. There's no account and no upload.

Can it go the other way, Word to PDF?

Yes, and that direction keeps the layout. The Converter has a full office engine built in, so Word, Excel and PowerPoint files become PDFs with their tables, columns and layout intact, offline.

Does it cost anything?

No. The Converter is free and open source.
